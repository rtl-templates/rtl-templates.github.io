How to do bookkeeping for a small business in a spreadsheet

1. Pick your categories once

Use a short list of income and expense categories that match your tax form and your business: sales, services, supplies, software, fees, marketing, travel. The spreadsheet comes with 24 common ones that you can rename.

2. Log every sale and expense the same week

One row per transaction: date, description, category, amount. Keep the receipt number in the notes column. Ten minutes a week is enough for most small businesses.

3. Check profit and loss every month

Income minus expenses, by category and by month. If a category grows faster than sales, you see it early.

4. Put tax money aside as you go

Set an estimated rate and the sheet tells you how much of each month's profit to move to a separate account, so tax season is not a surprise. Ask an accountant for your real rate.

5. Chase unpaid invoices

Write down every invoice with its due date. Anything overdue turns red, so you know who to follow up with.
